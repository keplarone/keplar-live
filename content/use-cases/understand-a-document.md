Long documents are hard because the important detail can be on page 17. An assistant can help you find the structure and the questions to ask. It cannot replace reading the parts that matter, and for contracts or financial documents it cannot replace a qualified professional.

## What to ask for

1. **Structure:** "List the main sections and what each one does."
2. **Plain-language summary** with exact numbers and conditions kept.
3. **Obligations:** "What must each party do, by when?"
4. **Unusual or one-sided terms,** termination, fees and penalties.
5. **What is missing,** which topics you would expect and don't see.
6. **Questions to ask** the author or a professional.

The [work prompts](/prompts/work) include a faithful-summary template that ends with a "left out" line, so you can see what the compression dropped.

## How to attach it

You can attach files to a question, and Keplar reads their contents to answer you. Text inside a document is treated as material to read, never as instructions to Keplar. Very large documents may hit size limits; if so, paste the relevant sections. See [Attachments, images and video](/docs/using-keplar/attachments-images-video).

## What a panel adds

Different models notice different clauses. When several read the same document, the Disagreements section can show that one flagged a termination clause the others did not mention. That is a pointer to read that clause yourself. A reviewer compares the draft with the individual responses, and the final answer weighs them.

## Checking the summary

Open the document to the parts that matter and confirm:

- numbers, dates and names;
- negations ("not", "unless", "except");
- who is responsible for what;
- anything the summary says is absent.

## Privacy

Documents can contain sensitive information. Redact names and identifiers when you can. On the Free plan, free model hosts may log prompts, so do not upload confidential documents there. Read [Free plan privacy](/docs/privacy-security/free-plan-privacy).

## Honest limits

Keplar is not a lawyer or an accountant and gives no legal or financial advice. Rules vary by place and date, and an AI summary can miss what is not written. For contracts, taxes and anything with significant consequences, use the output to prepare questions for a professional.
