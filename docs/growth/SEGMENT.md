# Segment choice: who Keplar should serve first, and for which job

Researched 3 Oct 2026 (US/UK/EU web sources, opened on that date unless noted). Nothing here is a Keplar customer result. **Where there is no public evidence the cell says "unknown" and the number is not guessed.**

## Decision
- **Primary segment: independent developers and technical solo founders** (people who build products alone or in a pair, choose their own stack, and have nobody to ask).
- **One recurring job: pressure-test a technical or product decision before committing to it** (database shape, auth approach, build-vs-rewrite, framework or library choice, a security question, pricing-page copy) by seeing *where several models agree and where they split*, so the split tells them what to verify.
- Why this and not the others is in the table and the notes below. It is a **hypothesis to test with real people** (see [RESEARCH-KIT.md](RESEARCH-KIT.md)), not a finding.

Not enough evidence to prefer it over developers.

## Competing tools for the "ask several models" intent (web search, 3 Oct 2026)
MultipleChat (multiplechat.ai/tools/ask-multiple-ai-models), Qorpus (qorpus.ai), Talkory (talkory.ai/compare-ai-answers), Aymo (aymo.ai/compare-ai-models), SmophyAI (smophy.ai/multichat) all show several models' answers side by side, some with a synthesized answer; MultiAsk is described as free and no-signup for up to five models. The idea is not unique; Keplar's differences to prove are: a verified single answer with the disagreement explained, free models with no setup, and honest limits. We have not tested these competitors; any comparison must be run and dated before it is published (see campaign/DEMO-RUNS.md rules).

## Honest caveats on the choice
1. **Developers are skeptical of AI and of marketing.** The same survey that shows demand shows distrust. Messaging must lead with the concrete benefit, show the disagreement view working, and state limits (no web lookups, agreement is not proof).
2. **Disagreement is not frequent.** In the first 9 live tests it appeared on 2 (see [BASELINE.md](BASELINE.md)); the pitch is "see where models split *when they do*", and the best prompts are trade-off decisions, not facts.
3. **Free models, not frontier models.** Free-plan answers come from free open models; a developer may judge Keplar on those. Say so.
4. **The audience is unmeasured.** No visit, signup or conversion data exists yet for any segment. The first 100 activated users are the evidence gate.
5. **Developer willingness to pay is unknown**; prices above single-model subscriptions are a risk (see [OFFER.md](OFFER.md)).
